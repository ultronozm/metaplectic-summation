Poisson summation on the plane gives the functional equation for Eisenstein series on $\mathrm{SL}_2$.  We develop an analogue of Poisson summation for Kubota's $n$-fold cover of $\mathrm{SL}_2$, in the spirit of Braverman--Kazhdan.  Locally, we define a Schwartz space consisting of genuine functions on Deligne's $\mu_n$-torsor over the punctured plane.  It comes with a Fourier transform $\mathcal{F}$ defined like the symplectic Fourier transform, but with the symplectic form replaced by its $n$th roots.  At almost all places, we define a basic vector fixed by $\mathcal{F}$.  Globally, we prove a Poisson summation formula for the resulting adelic Schwartz space.  Taking a Fourier coefficient gives a general Voronoi-type summation formula for Gauss sums of order $n$, with an explicit integral transform relating the weights.  For cubic Gauss sums, the formula takes sextic residue twists to quadratic twists, as in recent work on the quasi-Riemann hypothesis.

* Lean formalization
For number fields, the main results of the paper are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, in the directory [[https://github.com/ultronozm/metaplectic-summation/tree/main/lean][lean/]] of the repository.  The development has no ~sorry~, and the statements below depend only on the standard axioms ~propext~, ~Classical.choice~ and ~Quot.sound~.

The file [[https://github.com/ultronozm/metaplectic-summation/blob/main/lean/Challenge.lean][lean/Challenge.lean]] is a challenge in the format of the [[https://github.com/PalomarRegistry/PalomarTemplate][Palomar]] registry.  It states the Voronoi-type formula for twisted Gauss sums of order $n$ and its quadratic dual (the first two rows of the table below) against Mathlib alone, quantifying over self-dual measures and Hilbert symbols with their defining properties, and giving the local transform by its explicit integral.  The file [[https://github.com/ultronozm/metaplectic-summation/blob/main/lean/Solution.lean][lean/Solution.lean]] proves them from the development.  The Poisson formula, Fourier inversion and the other statements in the table are proved in the development itself.

Not formalized: the case of global function fields; the remark comparing the transform with Kubota's transform over $\mathbb{C}$; and, at archimedean places, the smoothness across $\mu = 0$ of the extensions of the normalized rays at the origin.
